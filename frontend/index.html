How Risk Is Determined

When you click Analyze Patent Risk, the system runs three steps:

  1. Concept extraction — your description is lowercased, stripped of punctuation and common English words (stopwords), then the most frequent single words and two-word phrases (bigrams) are extracted as technical concepts. Up to 8 key terms are surfaced and shown as the coloured pills above the results.
  2. Patent retrieval — when a PATENTSVIEW_API_KEY is configured, the system queries the PatentsView PatentSearch API (search.patentsview.org) using technical terms derived from your description. Up to 25 real patents are retrieved dynamically. Without a key, the API is not contacted and results are unavailable.
  3. Similarity matching — your full description is compared against the retrieved patents. Both the query and each patent's title + abstract are converted into TF-IDF vectors (term-frequency / inverse-document-frequency) using unigrams and bigrams, and cosine similarity is calculated. This measures vocabulary overlap, weighted by how distinctive each word is across the result set.
  4. Risk labelling — the cosine similarity score (0.0 – 1.0) is mapped to a risk level using fixed thresholds:
    High — score ≥ 0.20 (meaningful lexical overlap) Medium — score ≥ 0.08 (some shared terminology) Low — score < 0.08 (little to no overlap)

When live search is enabled, results come from the PatentsView database of US granted patents. The similarity labels (High / Medium / Low) reflect vocabulary overlap only — they are not legal infringement conclusions. Always consult a qualified patent attorney before making any legal or commercial decisions.
